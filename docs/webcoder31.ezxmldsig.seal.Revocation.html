    Class webcoder31\ezxmldsig\seal\Revocation

    The revocation status of the certificates of a chain, from CRLs and OCSP responses.

    Every certificate of the chain but its trust anchor needs a CRL of its issuer, or an OCSP response of its issuer or of a responder it delegated, that tells its status. Two questions are told apart:

    • which CRLs count: current ones, their next update not passed (a seal of level B, the time-stamping authority of a seal of level T), or ones issued after a time-stamp (the signer of a seal of level T or LT, and its time-stamping authority at level LT, whose CRLs are embedded and no longer current when the seal is verified);
    • which revocation makes the certificate unusable: any one, or one at or before the time-stamp only, the time-stamp proving that the seal existed before.

    The certificates of a time-stamping authority follow RFC 3161, 4: a revocation after the time-stamp leaves its tokens valid only if the CRL gives a reason that is not a compromise of a key. Without a reason, or for a key compromise, every token of the authority is distrusted.

    A CRL or a response issued after the certificate expired proves nothing of it, since a CA may drop the expired certificates from its CRLs and a responder forget them, unless they say they keep them (ExpiredCertsOnCRL, archive cutoff).
