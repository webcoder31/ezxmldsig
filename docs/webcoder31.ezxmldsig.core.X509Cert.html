    Class webcoder31\ezxmldsig\core\X509Cert

    Analyze an X.509 certificate.

    The X509Cert class provides a set of convenient methods for extracting essential information contained in an X.509 certificate and for performing some useful checks. Every signature of a certificate is verified by OpenSSL. isTrustedBy() leaves the whole chain of trust to OpenSSL, at the current date; getTrustedChain(), which the seals use at the time of a time-stamp, builds the chain and checks the dates and the constraints of the CAs itself, from the DER of the certificates, with the rules of OpenSSL. This class belongs to the core, shared by the tokens (XMLDSigToken) and the seals.

    public isTrustedBy(string|array $caCertPaths): boolean

    Check that the certificate is trusted: OpenSSL builds and verifies its chain up to a certificate of the given CA files.

    Check that the certificate is trusted: OpenSSL builds and verifies its chain up to a certificate of the given CA files.

    Every certificate of the given files is trusted, and the chain is built from these certificates only. OpenSSL verifies the signature of each link, the validity dates of every certificate of the chain at the current date, that each issuer is a CA (basic constraints, key usage, path length) and that the chain ends at a self-signed certificate of the given files. A chain that stops at an intermediate CA is rejected: to trust the certificates issued by an intermediate CA, give its certificate and the certificates up to its root (in one file or in several). A certificate issued by another intermediate CA of the same root is then rejected, since the chain cannot be built without that other intermediate certificate.

    public isValidCA(string $caCert): boolean

    Check the origin of the certificate.

    Check the origin of the certificate.

    Verify that the X.509 certificate has been issued by the given CA certificate: OpenSSL verifies the signature of the certificate with the public key of the CA certificate, the issuer name of the certificate must be the subject name of the CA certificate, and the CA certificate must be a CA ("CA:TRUE" basic constraint and, if it has a "keyUsage" extension, "keyCertSign").

    Only this link is checked: neither the validity dates nor the chain of the CA certificate. Use isTrustedBy() to verify the whole chain.

    NOTE: More than one CA certificate can give a positive result, some CAs re-issue signing certificates after having only changed the expiration dates. Note that it also works with self-signed CA certificates. In this case, pass the X.509 certificate itself to the function.

    public getTrustedChain(string|array $caCerts, array $untrusted = [], int|null $at = null): array|null

    The chain of the certificate, from it to its root, built and verified from the given CA certificates as OpenSSL does for isTrustedBy(), at the current date or at another one; NULL if the certificate is not trusted.

    The chain of the certificate, from it to its root, built and verified from the given CA certificates as OpenSSL does for isTrustedBy(), at the current date or at another one; NULL if the certificate is not trusted.

    Every given certificate is trusted, and the chain is built from these certificates only (and the $untrusted ones), never from those of the system. A certificate is taken as the issuer of another one when its subject is the issuer of the other one (names compared in the canonical form of OpenSSL), when the authority key identifier of the other one matches it, and when its key suits the signature algorithm of the other one. Then:

    • OpenSSL verifies the signature of each link (but the root's own);
    • every certificate of the chain, the root included, is valid at the date of the verification;
    • every issuer is a CA: basic constraints with "CA:TRUE" and, if it has a key usage, "keyCertSign"; the root may also be a CA by its key usage alone, or by being self-signed and of version 1;
    • the path length of each CA is respected;
    • no certificate has a critical extension the verification does not handle, nor a repeated or malformed extension.

    A chain that stops at an intermediate CA is rejected: the root must be one of the CA certificates. Name constraints are not enforced: a CA certificate that has them is rejected, where OpenSSL would enforce them.

    Parameters

    $caCerts  

    The CA certificates: paths of PEM files, or PEM contents, each of which may hold several certificates.
    $untrusted  

    Certificates (X509Cert) the chain may be built with, after the CA certificates, without being trusted: the intermediate CAs a signature embeds.
    $at  

    The UNIX time the certificates must be valid at; NULL for the current time.

    Returns

    The chain (X509Cert), or NULL.
