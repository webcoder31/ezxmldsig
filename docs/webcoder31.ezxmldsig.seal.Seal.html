    Class webcoder31\ezxmldsig\seal\Seal

    Seals of files: an ASiC-E container (ETSI EN 319 162-1) holding the files and one detached XAdES baseline signature over them (ETSI EN 319 132-1), at level B, T (a signature time-stamp, RFC 3161) or LT (the certificates, CRLs and OCSP responses that validate it, embedded). The Node.js library ezxmldsig-js creates and verifies the same seals; its design document, design/seal.md, gives the structure and the rules followed.

    The seals are an experimental feature of the 2.x versions: their API may change in a minor version.

    public static verifySeal(string $container, array $options): SealVerification

    Verify a seal.

    Verify a seal.

    The structure is read strictly: every element of the signature is where the baseline puts it, once, and anything this library does not process (a signature policy, a manifest of references, a transform) makes the seal invalid rather than ignored. A seal is valid only if every file of the container outside META-INF is covered, so that a file added to a sealed container cannot pass for a sealed one.

    The signing certificate is trusted at the time of the signature time-stamp, if any, and at the current date otherwise: SigningTime is claimed by the signer, and cannot date the check of its certificate. The certificate of the time-stamping authority is checked at the current date. The revocation of the certificates is checked, by CRLs or OCSP responses, unless 'revocation' is FALSE: the library sends no request, the data come from the seal (level LT) and from the options.

    $container  

    The ASiC-E container.

    Returns

    The result. An invalid seal does not throw: see its getError().

    On a misuse: the options are not valid, or a CA certificate cannot be read.

    public static addValidationData(string $container, array $options): string

    Add to a seal of level T the certificates, CRLs and OCSP responses that validate it: it becomes a seal of level LT (EN 319 132-1, 5.4, 5.5.1, 6.3), which a third party can validate without reaching the CAs. The answers are checked before they are embedded, only those that told the status of a certificate; they must be issued after the time-stamp. The signed parts of the signature file are left byte for byte as they are.

    Add to a seal of level T the certificates, CRLs and OCSP responses that validate it: it becomes a seal of level LT (EN 319 132-1, 5.4, 5.5.1, 6.3), which a third party can validate without reaching the CAs. The answers are checked before they are embedded, only those that told the status of a certificate; they must be issued after the time-stamp. The signed parts of the signature file are left byte for byte as they are.

    What goes where: xades141:TimeStampValidationData, right after the time-stamp, the certificates of the chain of the time-stamping authority the token does not hold, and their CRLs or OCSP responses; xades:CertificateValues, the certificates of the chain of the signer ds:KeyInfo does not hold, its trust anchor included; xades:RevocationValues, a CRL or an OCSP response for each certificate of that chain, its trust anchor excepted. Each one is embedded once.

    Parameters

    $container  

    The container.
    $options  

    'caCertPath' and 'tsaCertPath', as for verifySeal(), and at least one of 'crls' (in DER or PEM) and 'ocspResponses' (DER), fetched as validationDataRequests() lists.

    Returns

    The container, with the validation data in its signature.

    Throws

    Exception 

    If the seal is not valid, is not of level T, or if the data do not tell the status of every certificate after the time-stamp.
